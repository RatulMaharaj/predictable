# An IFRS 17 sample valuation

**Audience: an actuary deciding whether this framework is worth their time.** This page is
a basis note and a results commentary for a complete IFRS 17 general measurement model
valuation of a 10,000-contract portfolio, run end to end by
[`demos/ifrs17/run_demo.sh`](https://github.com/RatulMaharaj/predictable/blob/main/demos/ifrs17/run_demo.sh).

Every number below is produced by that script. Every one can be re-derived — from the
committed model, from a portfolio generated by one seed, and from the run's own manifest
digests — and the last section shows the arithmetic behind one contract's CSM, cell by
cell, as the engine actually evaluated it.

```console
$ ./demos/ifrs17/run_demo.sh
...
IFRS 17 DEMO: OK
```

---

## 1. What is being valued

| | |
|---|---|
| **Product** | unit-linked endowment with a death benefit and a maturity guarantee |
| **Measurement** | general measurement model (GMM), IFRS 17.32-.52 |
| **Portfolio** | 10,000 contracts in three annual cohorts (2024 / 2025 / 2026) |
| **Valuation date** | 30 June 2026 |
| **Projection** | 360 months, `origin = "policy"` |
| **Model** | `models/ifrs17_gmm/` — the committed reference model, **unmodified** |
| **Assumptions** | `models/ifrs17_gmm/base.pir` — the committed central basis |

The model is the reference model with no demo-specific edits. That is a deliberate
constraint: a sample valuation whose model was adjusted for the sample demonstrates the
sample. The only things this demo owns are the portfolio and the disclosure cuts in
`run.pir`.

The portfolio is synthetic and deterministic — one seed, `random` from the standard
library, regenerated on every run — so the `model_digest`, `run_digest` and
`results_digest` in the manifest are stable and mean something.

**On cohorts.** The projection runs on `origin = "policy"`: every contract is projected
from its own inception, and the cohort field carries the year of issue. Contracts written
in 2024 therefore enter the projection with two years of accumulated account value in
`initial_account`. Annual cohorts are an IFRS 17.22 requirement and they are what turn the
CSM roll-forward from a number into a table; they are not a re-basing of the projection
clock.

### The contract, in symbols

Per policy in force, in month `t`, with `A[t]` the account value:

```text
A[t] = (A[t-1] + alpha[t-1]*P - c[t-1]) * (1 + i_c_m)

c[t] = max(S - A[t], 0) * q[t] * lambda      cost of insurance
     + A[t] * a_m                            annual management charge
     + f[t]                                  policy fee
```

with `alpha[t]` the premium allocation rate (reduced in policy year 1), `i_c_m` the monthly
credited return, `S` the sum assured, `q[t]` the monthly mortality rate on a constant-force
basis, `lambda` the cost-of-insurance loading and `a_m` the monthly management charge.

Benefits are `max(S, A[t])` on death, `A[t] * (1 - penalty)` on surrender, and
`max(A[T], g * sum(P))` at maturity — the guarantee being a stated proportion `g` of the
premiums actually paid.

The order of operations inside a month (allocate, charge, then credit) changes the answer,
so it is written as one expression in one component rather than three, and any change to it
is a formula change to that component alone under `predictable diff model`.

## 2. The measurement model

### 2.1 Fulfilment cash flows

```text
FCF[0] = PV[death + surrender + maturity + expenses] + E_acq - PV[premiums]
```

discounted on a **term structure**, `v(t) = (1 + s_y)^(-t/12)`, where `s_y` is the spot rate
for policy year `y` read from `tables/yield_curve.csv` — not a flat rate. Each present value
is a separate named component (`pv_death_claims`, `pv_surrender_claims`, …) so that the
composition of the FCF is inspectable without opening the model.

Discounting respects timing automatically. `premium_income` is tagged `START` and
`death_claims` is tagged `END`, and `npv` applies each component's own timing when it
discounts, which is why the two are not discounted at the same factor for the same `t`. In
a system without timing tags this is a convention you have to remember to apply and to
document; here it is a consequence of a declaration the model could not omit.

### 2.2 Risk adjustment

```text
RA[0] = rho * PV[death + surrender + maturity claims],      rho = 4.5%
```

This is a **percentage loading, not a quantile**, and the write-up says so rather than
implying a confidence level that was never calibrated. It was chosen because it is
auditable in one line. A cost-of-capital or a quantile calibration replaces exactly this
one component and nothing downstream of it — which is the property worth having, and which
`predictable diff model` will confirm by naming `risk_adjustment` as the only formula change
and listing its transitive impact set.

### 2.3 CSM at initial recognition

```text
CSM[0] = max(-(FCF[0] + RA[0]), 0)
LC[0]  = max(  FCF[0] + RA[0],  0)
```

The two are the positive and negative parts of the same quantity, so exactly one of them is
non-zero for any contract: a contract is profitable **or** onerous, never both (IFRS
17.47-.48). The identity

```text
CSM[0] - LC[0] = -(FCF[0] + RA[0])
```

is checked at portfolio level by the demo and reported with its residual.

### 2.4 Coverage units and release

Coverage units are **sum assured in force per 1,000** (IFRS 17.B119). That is a policy
choice, appropriate here because the insurance service being provided is a death benefit; a
savings-dominant contract would more often use the account value. Units are discounted to
`t = 0` before being used as the release basis:

```text
u[t] = N[t] * S / 1000 * v(t)              coverage units provided in month t, discounted
U    = sum(u[t], t = 0..T)                 the release denominator  (a stage-2 reduction)
Uhat[t] = max(Uhat[t-1] - u[t-1], 0)       units still to be provided; Uhat[0] = U
R[t] = min(u[t] / max(Uhat[t], 1e-9), 1)   the fraction of remaining service given in t
```

`Uhat` is seeded from `U` through `init` — the one legal stage-2 back-channel — and `R[t]`
is the proportion of *remaining* service delivered in month `t`. The `max(..., 1e-9)` guard
is there instead of a branch: after run-off both numerator and denominator are zero, and the
guard keeps the quotient at zero rather than trapping.

### 2.5 The roll-forwards

```text
CSM[t]     = max((CSM[t-1] - CSM_rel[t-1]) * (1 + i_L_m), 0)
CSM_rel[t] = CSM[t] * R[t]
```

accreting at the **locked-in** rate `i_L = 3.8%` p.a. (IFRS 17.B72(b)), monthly-equivalent
`i_L_m = 1.038^(1/12) - 1`. The risk adjustment rolls forward identically. The fulfilment
cash flows roll forward retrospectively,

```text
FCF[t] = (FCF[t-1] - NO[t-1]) * (1 + i_L_m)
LRC[t] = FCF[t] + RA[t] + CSM[t]
```

with `NO[t]` the net outflow, and `LRC` the reported liability for remaining coverage.

### 2.6 Why this model is structurally interesting

IFRS 17 asks for quantities that depend on the **whole** projection (`U`, `FCF[0]`,
`RA[0]`, `CSM[0]`) and are then released **over** it. That is a loop, and the IR
refuses to close it implicitly. It is expressed as the one legal backward channel
(`01-ir.md` §8.2), used twice:

```text
stage 1   fulfilment cash flows, coverage units          Series
stage 2   their present values                           level 1  (an Agg)
stage 1   CSM / RA / FCF balances, seeded through init
stage 2   present values of what those balances released  level 2  (an Agg)
```

Two substage levels, no more — a third would be `W0110`. The practical consequence is that
the CSM is computed in a single pass over the portfolio rather than by iterating the model
to convergence, and that the dependency is *visible*: `predictable graph` shows the
`init` edge from `total_coverage_units` into `remaining_coverage_units` explicitly.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Initial recognition, by cohort

| cohort | contracts | FCF at issue | RA | CSM | loss component | onerous | LRC at issue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2024 | 2,815 | −327,140 | 1,749,901 | 427,226 | 1,849,987 | 1,895 | 1,849,987 |
| 2025 | 3,381 | −4,259,745 | 2,003,432 | 2,647,325 | 391,011 | 785 | 391,011 |
| 2026 | 3,804 | −8,404,611 | 1,967,266 | 6,457,001 | 19,656 | 118 | 19,656 |
| **total** | **10,000** | **−12,991,495** | **5,720,599** | **9,531,551** | **2,260,654** | **2,798** | **2,260,654** |

Read the cohort gradient before anything else, because it is the result with actuarial
content. The 2024 cohort is 67% onerous; the 2026 cohort is 3%. That is not a cohort effect
in the accounting sense — it is the portfolio construction showing through. Contracts
written earlier enter with a larger accumulated account value, the account is what the
sum at risk is netted against, and the cost-of-insurance charge is levied on the sum at
risk. A larger account therefore means a smaller charge base, less margin, and a contract
that is more likely to fail the `FCF + RA <= 0` test.

Whether that is a defensible economic story is a question about the synthetic portfolio, not
about the measurement. It is called out here because a sample valuation that presents an
onerous ratio without saying what drives it is inviting the reader to attribute it to the
standard.

**LRC at issue equals the loss component, exactly** — 2,260,654 on both sides, residual
0.0. No gain is recognised at initial recognition (IFRS 17.38): the profitable contracts
contribute nothing to the opening liability, because their entire margin is the CSM, and
the CSM is inside the LRC. This is the model's own audit check, published as the output
`lrc_at_issue` so that a reviewer can reconcile a run without opening the model.

### 3.2 CSM roll-forward — whole portfolio, first ten years

| year | opening | interest accreted | released | closing |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 9,531,551 | 340,980 | 1,014,148 | 8,858,383 |
| 2 | 8,858,383 | 318,002 | 896,787 | 8,279,599 |
| 3 | 8,279,599 | 297,560 | 825,470 | 7,751,688 |
| 4 | 7,751,688 | 278,566 | 775,503 | 7,254,752 |
| 5 | 7,254,752 | 260,512 | 736,115 | 6,779,149 |
| 6 | 6,779,149 | 243,144 | 702,245 | 6,320,048 |
| 7 | 6,320,048 | 226,336 | 671,537 | 5,874,847 |
| 8 | 5,874,847 | 210,006 | 643,125 | 5,441,727 |
| 9 | 5,441,727 | 194,101 | 616,256 | 5,019,573 |
| 10 | 5,019,573 | 178,592 | 590,283 | 4,607,881 |

The per-cohort version is `demos/ifrs17/out/csm_rollforward.csv`; the risk adjustment
roll-forward is `ra_rollforward.csv` and has the same shape.

**The "interest accreted" column is not a plug.** It is
`sum((CSM[t] - CSM_rel[t]) * i_L_m)` over the twelve months of the year,
computed from the model's own monthly recursion. The roll-forward's identity —
`closing = opening + accreted − released` — is therefore a *test*, and the residual it
leaves is reported in `reconciliations.json`: **-2.3e-9** at worst across all cohorts
and all thirty years. A roll-forward whose accretion line absorbs the balancing difference
reports the identity rather than testing it, and cannot tell you when the identity has
stopped holding.

Note also that year 1 releases 10.6% of the opening CSM while year 10 releases 11.8% of a
much smaller balance. The release fraction accelerates because coverage units run off with
the in-force, and the denominator `Uhat[t]` shrinks faster than the numerator.

### 3.3 The five reconciliations

| check | statement | residual | tolerance |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `lrc_at_issue = loss_component` | no gain at initial recognition (17.38) | 0 | 2.26 |
| `csm - loss_component = -(fcf + ra)` | the 17.47 dichotomy, at portfolio level | -1.1e-8 | 7.27 |
| `csm roll-forward closes` | opening + accretion − release = closing, every cohort, every year | -2.3e-9 | 0.01 |
| `csm fully released by t = T` | nothing left unrecognised at the end of coverage | 7.8e-9 | 0.01 |
| `service result = ra release + csm release` | no experience variance against the model's own basis | 0 | 21 |

The last one deserves a sentence. On the central assumption set the insurance service result
*must* be exactly the RA and CSM released, because expected claims equal incurred claims by
construction. It holds to 0.0 here, which is a statement about the model's internal
consistency and not about the world. Its value is as a **regression guard**: the day it
stops being zero, something has been introduced between the fulfilment cash flows and the
revenue presentation, and the residual will name the day.

Tolerances are scale-relative (`1e-6` of the quantity being checked) except for the two
roll-forward checks, which are absolute at one penny because they are checking a *closure*,
not a magnitude.

## 4. One contract's CSM, from the top

`UL000001` — male, 46 at entry, smoker, 30-year term, £125 a month, sum assured £24,535,
accumulated account £982.27, cohort 2025:

| quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| PV premiums | 13,779.81 |
| PV death claims | 1,832.82 |
| PV surrender claims | 6,077.86 |
| PV maturity claims | 3,023.04 |
| PV renewal expenses | 709.65 |
| acquisition expense | 450.00 |
| **FCF at issue** | **−1,686.44** |
| **RA at issue** (4.5% of PV claims) | **492.02** |
| **CSM at issue** | **1,194.42** |
| LRC at issue | 0.00 |
| total discounted coverage units | 2,704.70 |
| PV of the CSM released | 1,224.97 |
| PV of the RA released | 504.60 |
| PV of the insurance service result | 1,729.57 |

Check the arithmetic by hand:

```text
FCF[0] = 1,832.82 + 6,077.86 + 3,023.04 + 709.65 + 450.00 - 13,779.81 = -1,686.44
RA[0]  = 0.045 * (1,832.82 + 6,077.86 + 3,023.04)                     =    492.02
CSM[0] = max(-(-1,686.44 + 492.02), 0)                                =  1,194.42
LRC[0] = -1,686.44 + 492.02 + 1,194.42                                =      0.00
```

— no gain recognised at initial recognition, as required.

Note that the PV of the CSM released (1,224.97) exceeds the CSM at initial recognition
(1,194.42). That is not an error: the CSM accretes at the locked-in rate 3.8% while the
release is discounted on the yield curve, and the two are different rates. The gap is the
accretion the contract earns while it is waiting to be recognised.


Four things in that output are worth an actuary's attention.

1. **The recursion is visible as a recursion.** `csm[t=12]` unrolls into `csm[t=11]` into
   `csm[t=10]`, each with its own value, its own release and its own accretion factor. This
   is the audit request "show me how the CSM got from December to January" answered
   literally, at any `t`, without a bespoke extract.
2. **Every leaf says where it came from.** `locked_in_rate` is tagged
   `assumption[base]` — the assumption set it was read from — and `1.038 ^ 0.0833` is the
   monthly conversion shown as arithmetic rather than as a constant. Nothing in the tree is
   a number of unstated provenance.
3. **`N0202` is a note, not an error, and it is the interesting one.** `csm[t-1]` is
   `POINT`-timed (a balance at an instant) and `csm_release[t-1]` is `START`-timed (a flow
   over a period), and the model subtracts one from the other. That is correct here — the
   release is taken at the start of the month from the balance carried into it — but it is
   exactly the kind of timing assumption that is invisible in a spreadsheet, gets
   misremembered at the next handover, and moves the answer. The engine states it every
   time the cell is explained.
4. **The units carry through.** `money`, `factor`, `rate(annual)` are checked, not
   decorative: `qx / 12` where `1 - (1-q)^{1/12}` was meant is a unit error the checker
   refuses before the model ever runs.

The same trace is available for any of the 10,000 contracts, for any of the 47 components,
at any of the 361 timesteps — from the command line, from Python (`results.explain(...)`),
or from inside the governance pack in a browser with no engine installed.

## 5. The governance pack

```console
$ predictable export runs/valuation --out out/governance-pack.html \
      --modelpoints sample:50 --with-traces csm_release
out/governance-pack.html  11,543,012 byte(s), 50 modelpoint(s) × 47 component(s), manifest verified
```

One HTML file. No server, no network, no installation: the results travel as base64 Arrow
IPC inside the page, alongside the graph document, the run manifest with every input digest,
and pre-baked `explain()` traces for the sampled contracts. `manifest verified` on that
line means the pack was built from a run whose declared digests were re-checked against the
bytes on disk, not merely copied.

The demo asserts that the file contains no external `http(s)` reference, because a
governance artefact that phones out to a CDN is one DNS change away from being unopenable —
which is a bad property for something an auditor is expected to be able to read in seven
years.

`--modelpoints sample:50` is the first 50 in file order, deterministically, so re-exporting
the same run produces the same bytes. The whole book would be a 4 GB page; the sample is
the audit trail for a sample, and the manifest tells you what it is a sample *of*.

## 6. What this valuation is not

* **The RA is a loading, not a calibration.** §2.2. No confidence level is implied.
* **The guarantee is valued deterministically.** `pv_guarantee_cost` is the cost of the
  maturity guarantee *if the world does exactly what the central assumptions say*. On this
  basis it bites on 942 of the 10,000 contracts and costs 274,469 in present value — about
  2.9% of the CSM. That is the correct deterministic answer and the wrong answer to the
  question an actuary is actually asking about a guarantee, which is what it is worth
  across the distribution of outcomes. A stochastic or closed-form option valuation
  replaces that one component and nothing else.
* **The portfolio is synthetic.** Its onerous ratio is a property of the generator (§3.1),
  not a market observation.
* **The LIC carries a one-month settlement lag.** Short, and deliberately so: it is the
  smallest lag that makes the LRC/LIC split a real split rather than a relabelling.
* **No reinsurance, no VFA, no PAA.** This is the GMM on a direct portfolio.

Each of those is a component, not a rewrite. That is the claim the framework is making, and
`predictable diff model` is how you check it: change `risk_adjustment` and the structural
diff will tell you, before you run anything, exactly which outputs can move.

## 7. Reproducing

```console
$ ./demos/ifrs17/run_demo.sh                 # ~90 seconds, 10,000 contracts
$ N=1000 ./demos/ifrs17/run_demo.sh          # a quick pass
```

The run writes 101,270,000 result rows (≈600 MB) into `demos/ifrs17/runs/valuation/`; both
that directory and `out/` are ignored by git. The projection itself takes about three
seconds of wall time on four threads — the rest is Parquet.
